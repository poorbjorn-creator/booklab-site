The most anticipated nonfiction books coming out this month

Who is Bjorn?

I'm Bjorn - I've reviewed 300+ nonfiction books over the last 10 years on YouTube ( subscribers) and X ( followers). Every month I dig through the new releases so you don't have to. These are my honest picks. Eight this month, zero filler.

01
The Great Atom Debate: Ernst Mach, Ludwig Boltzmann, and the Battle for the Essence of Reality
Paul Halpern
Physics / History of Science / Nature of Reality December 1

In the late 1800s, two Austrian physicists fought over whether atoms were real. Paul Halpern tells the story of Mach and Boltzmann's clash, the human cost of settling it, and what it says about today's arguments over what is fundamental. 336 pages. Published by Basic Books.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "Everything is made of atoms. It sounds so obvious that you forget somebody had to prove it, and that serious physicists once called the idea a useful fiction. This is the moment physics had to decide whether to trust what it couldn't see. Halpern has written about Einstein and Schrödinger before, so I'm expecting the people to matter as much as the physics. If you liked Six Easy Pieces, this is the story behind its first principle."
Read if:
  • You liked Six Easy Pieces and want the story behind physics' first principle
  • You enjoy intellectual feuds where both sides had a point
  • You're curious how science decides what's real when nobody can see it

02
The Man Who Broke Reality: Niels Bohr and the Making of Modern Physics
Philip Ball
Biography / Physics / Quantum Mechanics December

Philip Ball's biography of Niels Bohr, who explained the nuclear atom and stood at the centre of quantum mechanics. It follows his long argument with Einstein over what physical reality means, and his later work in the atomic age. 352 pages. Published by University of Chicago Press.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "Halpern's book is about whether the atom is real. Ball's is about the man who looked inside it and found something that didn't behave like anything we understand. Bohr argued with Einstein for decades about what reality even means, and I want to understand that argument properly. Hawking was on last month's page, and Bohr feels like a step further back in the same story. Read it right after number one."
Read if:
  • Last month's Hawking biography left you wanting more of the people behind modern physics
  • You've never quite understood what Bohr and Einstein were arguing about
  • You like biographies where the ideas are as big as the person
Buy on Amazon → Read A Brief History of Time Review → Best Biographies →
03
The Co-Creation: How Earth Made Life and Life Made Earth
Olivia Judson
Science / Evolution / Earth History December 1

Evolutionary biologist Olivia Judson retells the history of life as a story of energy rather than genes. She splits Earth's history into five expansions (rock, light, oxygen, flesh, fire) and shows how life became a force that reshaped the planet itself. 560 pages. Published by Penguin Press.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "Most books on evolution start with the gene. Dawkins made that the default way of thinking for a generation, me included. Judson starts with energy instead, and treats the planet as a participant rather than a stage. It's a big claim, and at 560 pages she's clearly not rushing it. I want to see whether it holds up."
Read if:
  • The Selfish Gene shaped how you think about evolution
  • You want one big idea that ties geology, chemistry and biology together
  • You like long books that take their time over a grand argument
Buy on Amazon → Read The Selfish Gene Review →
04
The Brain at Twilight: The Neuroscience of Our Last Words and Moments
Tarvez Tucker
Neuroscience / Consciousness / Mortality December 8

A neurologist who works in neurological intensive care explains what happens in the brain at the edge of death. Tarvez Tucker mixes patients' stories with accessible neuroscience on consciousness, partial consciousness and the decisions families face at the end. Published by Columbia University Press.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "I've reviewed The Death of Ivan Ilyich and The Denial of Death, and both look at the end of life from the inside. Tucker comes at it from the bedside, as a neurologist working in intensive care. I expect it to be a hard read in places. I also expect it to be one of the more honest books on this list."
Read if:
  • When Breath Becomes Air stayed with you
  • You're curious what neuroscience can and can't say about consciousness at the end of life
  • You want a doctor's view of death that's empathetic, not clinical
Buy on Amazon → Read The Denial of Death Review → Books About Death →
05
God AI: What to Expect When Machines Outsmart Us
Toby Walsh
AI / Technology / Existential Risk December 1

Toby Walsh, a professor of artificial intelligence at UNSW with more than forty years in the field, asks whether superhuman AI will be the best thing that happens to us or the last. He walks through the history, weighs the risks and sets out what we can still do to steer it. Published by Melville House.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "I've read the doomers and the optimists, and Bostrom, Suleyman and Gawdat are all on the site. What I haven't had is someone with forty years in AI research telling me plainly what he thinks happens next. October's AGI Chronicles was about the race. This one is about where the race might end up."
Read if:
  • You've read Superintelligence or The Coming Wave and want a researcher's reality check
  • You followed October's AGI Chronicles and want the next question answered
  • You want to make up your own mind between boom and doom
Buy on Amazon → Read Superintelligence Review → AI Founders Books →
06
Hype Immunity: Timeless Lessons for Navigating the AI Revolution
Mike Horton
AI / Technology History / Leadership December 15

Mike Horton, a former US federal Chief AI Officer, argues that AI isn't the first technology to promise everything. He draws on 250 years of industrial history to give leaders a practical way to tell real value from hype.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "This is the counterweight to God AI. Walsh asks what happens if AI becomes everything it promises. Horton asks why so much of it won't, at least not the way the pitch decks say. I've worked in an industry that lives on hype cycles, and I've watched plenty of "this changes everything" tools come and go. A book that goes back to steam and electricity to make sense of AI is the kind of grounding I want."
Read if:
  • You're tired of AI headlines and want history instead of predictions
  • You lead a team that's being told to "adopt AI" and want a framework for it
  • You liked last month's The Telephone and want the same long view on today's technology
Buy on Amazon → Read The Coming Wave Review → AI Founders Books →
07
Rules of Thumb: 44 Principles to Live, Work, and Thrive By
Mikael Krogerus & Roman Tschäppeler
Thinking / Decision-Making / Mental Models December 8

From the authors of The Decision Book, a pocket-sized collection of 44 laws and principles that can't be proven scientifically but keep turning out to be true. Short chapters and illustrations make it a book to dip into rather than read cover to cover. 176 pages. Published by W. W. Norton.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "Every game studio I've worked in ran on rules of thumb nobody wrote down. Get one level playable before you build ten. The last ten percent takes half the time. This book writes them down for life in general. It's the lightest thing on the list, and that's the point: it's the one I'd actually keep on the desk. It pairs well with Toyama's The Art of Organizing Thoughts from last month."
Read if:
  • You liked The Decision Book or The 80/20 Principle
  • You want something to read five minutes at a time
  • You collect mental models
Buy on Amazon → Read The 80/20 Principle Review → Productivity & Focus Books →
08
Eleven Days: The Fascinating True Account of Agatha Christie's Mysterious Disappearance
Laura Thompson
Biography / Mystery / Literary History December 1

In December 1926, Agatha Christie vanished for eleven days. Christie biographer Laura Thompson retraces her movements, tests every theory and draws on conversations with Christie's daughter, Rosalind. The book lands days before the 100th anniversary of the disappearance on Dec 3. 224 pages. Published by St. Martin's Press.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "Something lighter to close the year. I don't read much literary biography, but a real mystery about the world's most famous mystery writer is hard to resist, especially a hundred years to the week after it happened. Thompson has already written a full Christie biography, so this is a short, focused book from someone who has spent years with the story. A good one for a dark December evening."
Read if:
  • You want a short true mystery for the holidays
  • You're a Christie fan and want the real story behind the eleven days
  • You like biography that reads like detective work
