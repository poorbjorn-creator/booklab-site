Best Nonfiction Releases — October 2025

Philosophy, money, intelligence, and machines

📅 October 2025 · 5 Books

Philosophy, money, intelligence, machines — a lot of different things this month. Ryan Holiday closes out his Stoic Virtues series with wisdom. The inside story of AI's exponential rise gets told through the people who built it. A Nobel Prize winner revisits behavioral economics in the age of algorithms. A sweeping synthesis asks how intelligence actually emerges. And Morgan Housel follows up The Psychology of Money with a book about the art of spending it.

I'm Bjorn — I've reviewed 300+ nonfiction books over the last 10 years on YouTube and X. Every month I dig through all the new releases so you don't have to. These are my honest picks.

Watch the full breakdown on YouTube

01
Wisdom Takes Work: Learn, Apply, and Repeat
Ryan Holiday
Philosophy / Stoicism / Self-Education October 21

The final book in Ryan Holiday's Stoic Virtues series — covering courage, discipline, justice, and now wisdom. Drawing on stories of ancient and modern figures alike — Marcus Aurelius, Seneca, Abraham Lincoln — Holiday shows how to cultivate wisdom through reading, self-education, and experience. He teaches us to listen more than we speak, to think with nuance, to ruthlessly question our own beliefs, and to develop a method of self-education.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "Ryan Holiday is probably the author I've read the most books from. The series so far has had its ups and downs — Discipline Is Destiny is one of my favorites, but Courage Is Calling kind of sucked, honestly. Right Thing, Right Now was mediocre. So we have highs and lows, and I hope he ends on a high note. Self-education is of course not something you're a stranger to — you're following the book club. We are all self-educating while reading nonfiction."
Read if:
  • You've been following Holiday's Stoic Virtues series and want the finale
  • You believe in self-education through reading (you're here, so yes)
  • You liked Discipline Is Destiny, Ego Is the Enemy, or Stillness Is the Key
  • You want practical Stoic philosophy, not academic Stoicism

02
The Scaling Era: An Oral History of AI, 2019–2025
Dwarkesh Patel with Gavin Leech
Technology / AI / Oral History October 8

The inside story of AI's exponential rise told through firsthand accounts of the engineers, founders, and philosophers who made it happen. Patel, known for his deep-dive interviews, captures the energy, the chaos, and the breakthroughs that turned AI from a lab curiosity to a world-altering force. Published by Stripe Press. 248 pages.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "This sounds like a more interview-style book, but it also has really the people that were there — the shakers and movers of the industry. If you pair this with The Emergent Mind at #4, you get a double feature: one about how the AI revolution happened, and one about how intelligence itself emerges. Perfect for the darker months."
Read if:
  • You want the AI revolution told by the people who built it
  • You enjoy oral histories and interview-driven narratives
  • You liked The Coming Wave or Scary Smart
  • You want to understand what happened between GPT-2 and now
Buy on Amazon →
03
The Winner's Curse: Behavioral Economics Anomalies, Then and Now
Richard H. Thaler & Alex O. Imas
Economics / Psychology / Behavioral Science October 21

The Nobel Prize–winning father of behavioral economics revisits his classic concepts in the light of today's algorithmic markets. Thaler and Imas explore how AI systems exploit the same cognitive biases that once plagued humans — but now at a planetary scale. Named one of the Financial Times's Best Books of 2025.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "I've always loved how behavioral economists explain the irrationality of people — including myself. Of course I've read Thinking, Fast and Slow by Daniel Kahneman, which is a landmark book if you have any interest in cognitive biases. This one pairs human psychology and machine logic with a twist — sounds like it could be a really interesting read."
Read if:
  • You want to see behavioral economics updated for the age of algorithms
  • You liked Thinking, Fast and Slow, Nudge, or The Psychology of Money
  • You're curious how AI exploits the same biases that trip up humans
  • You want a Nobel laureate's take on markets and machines
Buy on Amazon →
04
The Emergent Mind: How Intelligence Arises in People and Machines
Gaurav Suri & Jay McClelland
Neuroscience / AI / Psychology October 21

A sweeping synthesis of neuroscience, psychology, and AI research that asks the fundamental question: how does intelligence emerge? The authors claim that the same processes that create human thought apply to the world of machines. A book that stretches your brain in all directions.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "I like a book that stretches my brain in all directions, and this sounds like one of those. You can see a theme this month — all the books except maybe Ryan Holiday's are tied to the AI revolution in one way or another. If you pair this with The Scaling Era at #2, you get a double feature about how the brain — both human and artificial — evolves."
Read if:
  • You want to understand the parallels between human and artificial intelligence
  • You liked The Beginning of Infinity, Superintelligence, or Behave
  • You're curious whether machine intelligence is fundamentally different from ours
  • You want neuroscience and AI research woven together
Buy on Amazon →
05
The Art of Spending Money: Simple Choices for a Richer Life
Morgan Housel
Finance / Psychology / Behavior October 7

From the author of The Psychology of Money comes a powerful companion volume on the why behind how we spend. Housel argues that money isn't just a number — it's emotion, identity, a story. Through sharp storytelling and behavioral insights, he reveals how our values, not our income, determine whether we feel rich or poor. This book isn't about getting rich — it's about getting the most out of what you have.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "Morgan Housel is an author I first encountered with The Psychology of Money — a great and accessible book about how people think about money and why, based on our childhood experiences, a lot of that thinking is irrational. His follow-up Same as Ever was even better — it talks about the cyclical nature of the world, and how recognizing these cycles is usually more important than following all the new trends. I think I will add this to my list as well because I really enjoy this author."
Read if:
  • You liked The Psychology of Money or Same as Ever
  • You want to understand why you spend the way you do
  • You're interested in the emotional side of money, not just the math
  • You want behavioral finance that reads like storytelling
