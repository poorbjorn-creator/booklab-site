Best Nonfiction Releases — September 2025

Social status, the birth of the web, disruptive innovations, and the logic of common knowledge

📅 September 2025 · 4 Books

Things are starting to heat up after summer. This month we have humanity's most powerful invisible force — social status. A memoir from the man who invented the World Wide Web. Eleven innovations that rewrote the rules of civilization. And Steven Pinker on the hidden logic of how we think about what everyone else is thinking. Four books, all worth your time.

I'm Bjorn — I've reviewed 300+ nonfiction books over the last 10 years on YouTube and X. Every month I dig through all the new releases so you don't have to. These are my honest picks.

01
Anointed: The Extraordinary Effects of Social Status in a Winner-Take-Most World
Toby Stuart
Psychology / Sociology / Economics September 2

A leading organizational theorist takes us deep into the realm of humanity's most powerful invisible force — social status — and how it shapes everything from who we trust to what we value to which ideas and innovations change the world and who gets credit for their success. With compelling examples from economics, fashion, art, and beyond, Stuart shows how social status creates winners and losers in our world.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "I thought about this the other week when I went to a wedding. There was this quite famous person holding the wedding, and for as much as I don't want to identify as someone who cares about social status — this person really stood out. Everyone noticed when they walked into the room. It's a really interesting phenomenon — the halo effect. That's why I wanted to put this book on the list."
Read if:
  • You've noticed how status silently shapes who gets attention and credit
  • You're interested in the halo effect and invisible social forces
  • You liked The Laws of Human Nature or The Righteous Mind
  • You want to understand why winner-take-all dynamics keep accelerating

Pick of the Month 02
This Is for Everyone: The Unfinished Story of the World Wide Web
Tim Berners-Lee
Memoir / Technology / History September 9

A memoir from the creator of the World Wide Web. Born in the same year as Steve Jobs and Bill Gates, Tim Berners-Lee famously distributed his invention for no commercial reward at all. Its widespread adoption changed everything, transforming humanity into the first digital species. The book promises to be an intimate memoir about how his inventions spread creativity and collaboration — while also imperiling truth and privacy in many ways.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "I'm due for a memoir. I want to really know more about the origins of the internet as it becomes more ambiguous and just everywhere. We're so fully entrenched in this communication technology — it's nice to go back to the roots and know how it came about. The guy is supposed to be quite humorous and funny, and I'd love to hear these anecdotes. The story of the internet, how it came about — that's something I want to read more about."
Read if:
  • You want to understand how the internet actually came to be
  • You're interested in the people behind the foundational protocols of digital life
  • You liked Steve Jobs, The Shallows, or Futureproof
  • You want a tech memoir with humor and humanity

03
Epic Disruptions: 11 Innovations That Shaped Our Modern World
Scott D. Anthony
Innovation / History / Technology September 16

A skeptical look at how eleven destructive innovations — including the iPhone, the transistor, disposable diapers, and Julia Child's The Art of French Cooking — reshaped industries and societies, propelling humanity toward new frontiers. From gunpowder to generative AI, Anthony weaves together the fascinating stories behind the world's most transformative disruptions.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "If you've been following this channel, you know I've been very interested in disruption and technologies recently. I've been reading up on energy and electricity — that's one hell of a disruptive technology in Energy and Civilization. We've talked about Bitcoin and its possible monetary disruption. I'm also currently reading The Structure of Scientific Revolutions about paradigm shifts. I want to be able to better recognize disruption when it happens — maybe if you can learn about the history of disruption, it's a little easier to spot where we're heading."
Read if:
  • You want to understand the patterns behind world-changing breakthroughs
  • You're interested in how disruption actually works — not just the buzzword
  • You liked Energy and Civilization, The Structure of Scientific Revolutions, or Zero to One
  • You want historical context for the AI disruption happening right now
Buy on Amazon →
04
When Everyone Knows That Everyone Knows...: Common Knowledge and the Mysteries of Money, Power, and Everyday Life
Steven Pinker
Psychology / Philosophy / Social Science September 23

Steven Pinker explains how we think about each other's thoughts about each other's thoughts — ad infinitum. It sounds impossible, but he shows that we do it all the time. This awareness, which we experience as "the public" or "out there," is called common knowledge, and it has a momentous impact on our social, political, and economic lives. Pinker shows how the hidden logic of common knowledge can make sense of financial bubbles, revolutions that come out of nowhere, why people hoard toilet paper, and why nervous lovers can't say goodbye at the end of a phone call.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "I've read Steven Pinker before — one of the first books I read when I started reading nonfiction was The Better Angels of Our Nature. I remember being just spellbound by it. So I want to highlight his new book here. Why do people hoard toilet paper at the first sign of an emergency — we all saw that during COVID. Why are Super Bowl ads filled with crypto ads? Why did Russian authorities arrest protesters carrying a blank sign? Constantly riveting in explaining the paradoxes of human behavior."
Read if:
  • You want to understand the hidden logic behind collective behavior
  • You're fascinated by financial bubbles, panics, and crowd psychology
  • You liked The Righteous Mind, The Black Swan, or The Selfish Gene
  • You want Pinker at his most playful and puzzle-solving
