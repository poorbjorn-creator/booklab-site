The Expedition: A Love Story

A hydrogen balloon, a doomed Arctic expedition, and one woman's decades-long obsession with finding out what really happened

by Bea Uusma

Andrée's Arctic Balloon Expedition

In 1897, three Swedish scientists set off for a polar expedition in a hydrogen balloon. Thirty years later they are found, by accident, dead on a deserted island. What happened to them and why did they die?

The author, Bea Uusma, gets obsessed by the subject and spends decades trying to find out what really happened. This is her account of what happened and the journey to uncover the last missing pieces of the puzzle.

What I really loved about this book is how each chapter uses very different narrative tools - diary entries from the crew, charts of data, maps, test results and research journals. This makes you feel like you are part of an ongoing mystery investigation.

Notes from the Ice

🎈 The hydrogen balloon leaked gas from the start. It was expected to last 30 days but was useless after a day or two.
🗺️ Sea charts of the Arctic region are just white. Page after page. Nothing exists there.
🐻 Eating polar bear liver can lead to vitamin A poisoning. The crew knew this and avoided it. The same goes for seals - but this they didn't know.
🏚️ After spending two weeks building a hut, the ice cracked underneath it and it had to be abandoned.
🐾 Polar bears can attack unprovoked. They can wander 100km a day on ice and smell seals from 30km away.

💡 Key Takeaway

Freud mentions three main sources of human suffering: the external environment, our aging body, and other people. This book reminds me of the relentless and brutish traits of nature untamed. Civilization (and with it, other people) might be a cheap price to pay for not having death lurking around every corner.

⚖️ Verdict

A completely unique reading experience. Uusma turns a 130-year-old mystery into something that feels alive and urgent. The way she mixes formats - data charts, diary entries, her own obsessive research - makes it feel less like a book and more like a case file you can't put down. If you have any interest in exploration, survival, or Swedish history, this is essential.
