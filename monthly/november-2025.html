Best Nonfiction Releases — November 2025

Solitude, sentimentality, and the price of the attention economy

📅 November 2025 · 3 Books + 1 Bonus

A short but sharp list this month. A book about the value of being alone — something I was craving at the time. A millennium-long history of human emotion that works as the perfect palate cleanser. And a deep look at how tech platforms went from promising freedom to extracting everything from us. Plus a bonus dystopian novel from 2016 that's getting eerily close to reality.

I'm Bjorn — I've reviewed 300+ nonfiction books over the last 10 years on YouTube and X. Every month I dig through all the new releases so you don't have to. These are my honest picks.

Pick of the Month 01
The Joy of Solitude: How to Reconnect with Yourself in an Overconnected World
Robert J. Coplan
Psychology / Neuroscience / Well-being November 4

Solitude is a part of the human experience — but just like any other relationship, your relationship with solitude can be satisfying and intimate, or it can leave you stuck in a cycle of sadness, anxiety, or anger. Coplan draws from psychology, neuroscience, literature, and sociology to guide readers through solitude's many dimensions and its effects on mental health and well-being. 272 pages.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "This one stood out to me because I'm a bit of a loner — or at least I enjoy time with myself. That's usually time for focus, reflection, and doing projects. But with work and family, the weeks can just fly by and you've had basically no time alone. I've been taking lunch by myself at work just to get an hour of downtime. November is a good month for solitude."
Read if:
  • You're craving alone time but can't seem to find it
  • You want a science-backed guide to making solitude work for you
  • You liked The Stranger in the Woods, Walden, or Deep Work
  • You're interested in the psychology of introversion and alone time
Buy on Amazon →
02
Soft: A Brief History of Sentimentality
Ferdinand Mount
History / Culture / Emotion November 18

A millennium-long history of emotion told through delightful snapshots, often mischievous storytelling, and a masterly command of history. Mount explores the shifting importance societies have placed on empathy and the misfortune of others. Each seismic moment — from the French Revolution to civil rights — has had a corresponding sentimental revolution that fueled great political turning points and came to define human civilization.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "This is a topic I've not seen covered before in any of these lists. How people in the past expressed their grief and joy — the shifting importance of emotion throughout history. Sounds like the perfect palate cleanser if you're stuck in one topic and want something completely different. A masterly command of history — I'm in."
Read if:
  • You want a palate cleanser that's unlike anything else on your shelf
  • You're curious about how emotion has shaped political revolutions
  • You liked The Righteous Mind or The Laws of Human Nature
  • You enjoy cultural history told with wit and personality
Buy on Amazon →
03
The Age of Extraction: How Tech Platforms Conquered the Economy and Threaten Our Future Prosperity
Tim Wu
Technology / Economics / Society November 2025

Our world is dominated by a handful of tech companies. They provide great conveniences and entertainment, but also stand as some of the most effective instruments of wealth extraction ever invented — seizing immense amounts of money, data, and attention from all of us. Tim Wu, the scholar and former White House official who coined the phrase "net neutrality," tells the story of an internet that promised widespread wealth and democracy in the '90s, only to create new economic classes and aid the spread of autocracy instead.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "Right up my alley. The internet is not in its best shape these days. I remember back in the late '90s and early 2000s — the internet was just anarchy, creativity, and expressions of freedom. The wild west. I kind of miss that sometimes. This also reminds me of This Is for Everyone by Tim Berners-Lee — the inventor of the worldwide web and the hyperlink. His memoir about the early days of the internet really brought me back to that time. Also reminds me of a Swedish book called The Netocrats — about how technology plays its own hand. The clock was invented so monks would know when to pray. But that little device now runs our lives."
Read if:
  • You remember the early internet and want to understand what happened
  • You're critical of Big Tech but want the argument backed by scholarship
  • You liked The Shallows, This Is for Everyone, or The Sovereign Individual
  • You want to understand how the attention economy actually extracts wealth
Buy on Amazon →
BONUS
The Mandibles: A Family, 2029–2047
Lionel Shriver
Dystopian Fiction / Economics / Society Originally published 2016

A dystopian novel set between 2029 and 2047, following a family living through the slow fall of the US empire. The dollar is crashing. The US defaults on its debt. Nations create a new currency called the "bancor." What makes this book remarkable is how much of what Shriver wrote in 2016 is starting to happen — the national debt, the BRICS nations exploring alternative currencies, the fragility of American economic dominance.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "This book is really blowing my mind. A lot of the things written in 2016 are actually starting to happen, which is both scary and interesting. There's some interesting macro stuff going on with the dollar and with debt — when I reviewed the debt book a few weeks ago it was at 37 trillion, and just months later it was 38 trillion. An insane pace. If you want a dystopian novel that hits close to home and is very close to reality, this is it. Digestible for the nonfiction minds as well."
Read if:
  • You're interested in what happens when a superpower's debt becomes unsustainable
  • You want fiction that feels more like prediction than imagination
  • You liked The Fourth Turning, The Bitcoin Standard, or The Sovereign Individual
  • You want a novel that pairs well with macro-economic nonfiction
