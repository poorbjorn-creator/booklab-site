📅 October 2026

The most interesting nonfiction books coming out this month

📅 October 2026 · 10 Books

October is stacked, and unusually coherent. A book arguing that reading itself is dying. Two books that flatly disagree about whether creativity is real or a con. The inside story of the AGI race. The Gold Rush that cracked the republic. And the tenth anniversary of the book that started the modern Stoic revival.

Most months I pick five or six. This time I couldn't cut below ten without losing something that mattered. That's either a great sign for October or a terrible sign for my reading queue.

Last updated: August 11, 2026 · This is an early preview — the final list, Pick of the Month, and video will be published as we get closer to October. Books may be added or swapped.

I'm Bjorn — I've reviewed 300+ nonfiction books over the last 10 years on YouTube (17k subscribers) and X (45k followers). Every month I dig through the new releases so you don't have to. These are my honest picks. Ten this month, zero filler.

01
The New Dark Ages: The Death of Reading and the Dawn of the Post-Literate Society
James Marriott
Culture / Media Theory / Human Nature October 6

A Times (London) columnist argues that collapsing literacy is the defining cultural shift of our era. Rising literacy built the modern world — revolutions, individual rights, scientific truth. Now reading is in free fall, university students can't finish assigned books, and the chain of knowledge linking reader to reader is breaking for the first time since Rome fell. A defence of long-form argument against the culture of the screen. 208 pages.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "This one is personal. I've spent ten years telling people to read books, and the trend line is going the wrong direction. The Shallows made the case that the internet is rewiring our brains — Marriott is saying the damage is done and we're living in the aftermath. A book about the death of reading, published for readers, reviewed on a channel about why books matter. That's either the most BookLab thing imaginable or the most depressing. Probably both."
Read if:
  • You've noticed your own attention span shrinking and want to understand why
  • You care about what we lose when a culture stops reading
  • You want a short, urgent book that makes the case for the thing you already do

02
Sleeping Beauties: Why Good Ideas Go Dormant and How They Wake Up
David Byrne
Creativity / History of Ideas / Human Potential October 6

The Talking Heads founder on ideas that arrived too early — dark matter, continental drift, solar panels, the steam engine, Vermeer, Melville. Why do breakthroughs get ignored, and what wakes them up? Usually an outsider from another discipline who breaks into the silo. A rejection of the meritocracy-of-ideas myth. 400 pages. Published by Penguin Press (UK: Canongate, Oct 8).

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "David Byrne is one of those people who thinks sideways — How Music Works changed how I think about creativity as a system, not a spark. This sounds like the optimistic case: great ideas exist, they just get buried by bad timing or bad luck. But here's what makes this month interesting — read Byrne, then read Frank at #3 two weeks later. Byrne says rescue the ignored ideas. Frank says the whole creativity worship is a scam. Same subject, opposite verdict. That's the kind of tension I live for."
Read if:
  • You're fascinated by why some ideas succeed and others vanish
  • You want a creative polymath's take on what we're losing without noticing
  • You want to read it against Frank at #3 — same subject, opposite verdict
Buy on Amazon →
03
The Creativity Con
Thomas Frank
Culture / Economics / Systems of Power October 20

An indictment of America's obsession with creativity and innovation — how these beloved ideas came to dominate political and economic conversation while masking inequality and protecting elite interests. From the author of What's the Matter with Kansas? Byrne at #2 says rescue the ignored ideas. Frank says the worship of ideas is the racket. Same subject, opposite verdicts, two weeks apart.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "This is the kind of book I live for — taking a sacred cow and butchering it in public. Everyone worships creativity and innovation. What if that worship is itself a tool of power? It's the same move Eric Hoffer made in The True Believer — showing that the thing everyone assumes is good might be something else entirely. Read it right after Byrne at #2 for maximum cognitive dissonance. Same subject, opposite verdict, two weeks apart. This pairing is what makes October interesting."
Read if:
  • You're suspicious of the "innovate or die" mantra in business and culture
  • You want to read it right after Byrne for maximum cognitive dissonance
  • You want a political economist's take on why "creativity" became a religion
Buy on Amazon →
04
The AGI Chronicles: The Inside Story of the Race to Create an Artificial Superintelligence
Kevin Roose
Technology / AI / Existential Risk October 6

150+ interviews and unpublished internal documents from inside OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind. New detail on the disagreements that led Anthropic's founders to leave OpenAI, and fresh reporting on Altman's firing and reinstatement. Starred Publishers Weekly review; blurbed by Isaacson and Kurzweil. 448 pages. Published by FSG.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "I reviewed Futureproof and Roose is a solid tech writer — clear-eyed, not a cheerleader. I work with AI every day and I'm deeply curious about what's actually happening inside these labs. This continues the AI thread from September's page — Socher vs Turkle was the philosophical debate, this is the war correspondent's report. The starred PW review and Isaacson blurb tell me this might be the definitive AI book of the year."
Read if:
  • You want the inside story of the AGI race from someone who was in the room
  • You want to understand what's coming next — not what already happened
  • You followed September's Socher-vs-Turkle AI debate and want the behind-the-curtain sequel
Buy on Amazon → Read Futureproof Review → AI Founders Reading List →
05
Vibe
Adam Grant
Psychology / Connection / Human Nature October 13

On the loneliness crisis — what happens when face-to-face interaction moves behind screens and communication gets outsourced to AI. Grant digs into the science of chemistry and closeness, and how to build bonds faster and stronger.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "I reviewed Think Again and Grant is a reliable writer — clear, well-researched, and not afraid to challenge his own audience. The loneliness crisis connects to everything I care about: Lost Connections, Attached, even Flow. What I'm curious about is whether Grant has something genuinely new to say or whether this is a well-packaged summary of what we already know. Either way, the topic matters."
Read if:
  • You've felt the pull of screens replacing real conversation
  • You want the science behind why some people click and others don't
  • You're interested in the loneliness epidemic beyond the headlines
Buy on Amazon → Read Think Again Review → Explore Human Nature Books →
06
You Don't Need a Smartphone
August Lamm
Attention Economy / Digital Minimalism October 6

A burnt-out social media influencer sold her smartphone for a dumb phone. Her mental health improved, her attention span came back, her curiosity returned. Now a manifesto for doing the same. Short, cheap (paperback, $21), and unapologetically prescriptive. The prescriptive counterpart to Marriott's diagnosis at #1.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "Deep Work made the intellectual case for protecting your attention. This makes the personal case — someone who actually did it and documented what happened. I use my phone constantly for BookLab, and I'm not sure the tradeoff is always worth it. Pairs with Marriott at #1: he says reading is dying, she says here's one reason why — and here's the fix. Diagnosis and prescription, same month."
Read if:
  • You've thought about downgrading your phone but haven't pulled the trigger
  • You want a short, practical book — not another 400-page treatise
  • You read Marriott at #1 and want the prescription after the diagnosis
Buy on Amazon →
07
Blockers: Rebels in the Deep State
Michael Lewis
Politics / Narrative Nonfiction / High-Agency October 6

Lewis found six career civil servants targeted by DOGE — one who kept Americans' tax data out of the wrong hands, one who made sure politicians played by the rules, one who worked out how to stop wildfires reaching suburbs, one who helped unravel cystic fibrosis. And a security guard. People who believed they had a calling and discovered they were no longer wanted. Audiobook exclusive to blockers.fm for three weeks via Pushkin. Published by W.W. Norton.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "Michael Lewis is one of the best narrative nonfiction writers alive. The Fifth Risk already made the case that the boring parts of government are the most important parts — and that was before DOGE showed up with a sledgehammer. Lewis doesn't do ideology. He finds the human story inside the system and makes you care about people you didn't know existed. Pairs with Philbrick at #8 — institutions under strain, 2025 and 1848. Same question, different century."
Read if:
  • You want the DOGE story told by a master storyteller, not a pundit
  • You liked The Big Short, The Fifth Risk, or Going Infinite
  • You're interested in what happens when institutions face a wrecking ball
Buy on Amazon →
08
The Rush: California Gold, the Civil War, and the Making of the Modern World
Nathaniel Philbrick
History / Power & Ambition October 6

January 1848, a carpenter spots gold at Sutter's Mill and triggers the largest voluntary migration in US history. Philbrick turns the Gold Rush into the origin story of modern American contradictions — capital consolidating at speed, democracy in thrall to private power, xenophobia weaponised in the name of liberty — and shows how it lit the fuse to civil war. From the author of In the Heart of the Sea and Mayflower. Published by Viking.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "I'm a sucker for well-told history, and Philbrick is one of the best in the business. The Gold Rush is one of those events everyone knows about but nobody really understands — the greed, the violence, the way it reshaped an entire nation in a few years. Capital consolidating at speed, democracy bending to private power — sound familiar? That's why it pairs with Lewis at #7. One is 1848, the other is now. Same structural question."
Read if:
  • You want history that reads like an adventure novel
  • You liked In the Heart of the Sea, Empire of the Summer Moon, or Killers of the Flower Moon
  • You're interested in the moments that made (or broke) America
Buy on Amazon →
09
Cormac McCarthy: A Legacy Revisited
Tracy Daugherty
Literary Biography / Intellectual History October 6

From the biographer of Didion, Heller, and Larry McMurtry. McCarthy chose his work over almost everything else, and this details what that cost him in marriages, friendships, and family. Starred Kirkus. Doesn't flinch from the late-life controversies. 528 pages. Published by St. Martin's Press.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "McCarthy is one of those writers where the biography might be as interesting as the novels. He lived like a hermit, turned down almost every interview, and wrote some of the most brutal and beautiful prose in American literature. Blood Meridian and The Road are books that change you. I'm curious how Daugherty handles a subject who didn't want to be known — and a starred Kirkus for a literary biography is a strong signal that he pulled it off."
Read if:
  • You've read McCarthy and want to understand the man behind the prose
  • You liked biographies of reclusive artists who chose the work over everything else
  • You believe great art comes from somewhere dark and you want to see where
Buy on Amazon → Best Biographies →
10
The Daily Stoic: 10th Anniversary Edition
Ryan Holiday & Stephen Hanselman
Philosophy / Stoicism / Self-Mastery October 6

Ten years and three million copies on, the book that launched the modern Stoic revival returns updated and expanded — new introduction, bonus chapters, new illustrations, 366 daily entries drawing on Marcus Aurelius, Seneca, and Epictetus. 416 pages. Published by Portfolio.

🎙️ Bjorn's take: "I've reviewed more Ryan Holiday books than any other author — Ego Is the Enemy, Stillness Is the Key, Discipline Is Destiny, Courage Is Calling, Right Thing, Right Now. The Daily Stoic was my entry point to his work and to practical Stoicism in general. Ten years later, Stoicism is everywhere — and this book is a big reason why. If you haven't read it, start here. If you have, the anniversary edition is a good excuse to revisit."
Read if:
  • You want a daily Stoic practice without reading Marcus Aurelius in the original
  • You've read the original and want to see what a decade of reflection adds
  • You've never read it — this is the entry point millions of readers used
