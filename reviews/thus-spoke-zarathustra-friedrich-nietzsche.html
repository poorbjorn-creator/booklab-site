Thus Spoke Zarathustra

A Book for All and None — Nietzsche's prophetic masterpiece on becoming more than human.

by Friedrich Nietzsche
"Man is a rope stretched between animal and the Übermensch — a rope over an abyss."

The Death of God and What Comes After

The death of God concerns Nietzsche because he has the foresight to see the chaos that might follow — a value system collapsing and a confused age where people can no longer tell up from down.

The solution he proposes is the Übermensch, the next step in human evolution. But he feared we might instead see a transformation of man into what he calls the Last Man.

🔑 The Übermensch

To become the Übermensch, one must transcend the established morals and prejudices of human society to define your own purpose and values in life. This is a person who is willing to risk it all for the advancement of humanity.

🔑 The Last Man

The Last Man is what Nietzsche feared would become of western man. Imagine a person laying on a couch with a bag of chips balanced on a potbelly, totally immersed in a game of Candy Crush after a long Pornhub session. Despite pleasures and comfort, the person is empty and miserable. There you have the Last Man.

📚 The Book

The book is written in a biblical style and is full of parables, analogies and mythical imagery. We get to follow a prophet-like character named Zarathustra who has isolated himself in a cave on a mountain top for 10 years. He grows weary of his wisdom and descends into humanity to tell the world what he had learned.

"To give birth to a dancing star you need chaos in your soul."

Nietzsche wrote the book in a 10-day burst of creative madness — "and it is clear that he didn't revise his work very carefully." And it took me 2 months to read it. 😬

Blood and Skin in the Game

"Of all that is written I love only what a man has written with his blood. Write with blood and you will find that blood is spirit."

Nietzsche believed the most convincing words were written in blood. You can trust someone who has skin in the game because at least you can assume that what they are doing is lining up with what they are saying.

Think of it as a metaphor: you can look like a sun but actually be a moon. The sun radiates its own light while the moon only reflects the light the sun already produced. It's easy to learn and recite what others have come up with before — but that's weak. You need to produce your own ideas and not just copy. Be a sun and not a moon. Have skin in the game and not just observe what others are doing.

Trees, Roots, and Evil

"But it is the same with man as with the tree. The more he seeks to rise into the height and light, the more vigorously do his roots struggle earthward, downward, into the dark, the deep — into evil."

This reminds me of Carl Jung's quote about the roots. The higher you climb, the deeper your shadow must reach. Growth isn't clean — it demands that you confront what's underneath.

Equality and the Tarantulas

"Thus I speak to you in a parable, you who make the soul giddy, you preachers of equality! To me you are tarantulas and the secretly vengeful."

The cry for equality is soured conceit, repressed envy — a want of revenge, Nietzsche seems to say. Inspired not by the heart but by revenge.

The Stillest Hours

"The greatest events — they are not our noisiest but our stillest hours. The world revolves, not around the inventors of new noises, but around the inventors of new values; it revolves inaudibly."

💡 Key Takeaway

The hardest book I've read to date — and a good preparation for tackling the Bible after summer. Some of Nietzsche's passages hit you like a freight train. Others leave you utterly lost. That's the deal you make when you open this book. (If you want the longer case for that kind of reading, see Why You Should Read Books That Confuse You.)

⚖️ Verdict

Just like a paralyzed person needs a stairlift to get to the top floor, I needed assistance to ascend to the vicinity of Nietzsche's ideas. Countless YouTube videos by professors and armchair philosophers were needed for me to gain a base level understanding of this book. And I cherished those lectures, more than I enjoyed reading the book.

It is not an easy read. But some parts of it are so powerful they'll leave you awestruck.
